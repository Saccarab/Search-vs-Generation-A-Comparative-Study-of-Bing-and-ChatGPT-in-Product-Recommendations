% Abstract
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This thesis investigates the grounding behavior of large language models (LLMs) in commercial product recommendations, examining how ChatGPT and Gemini select and cite web sources when answering high commercial intent queries drawn from real user conversations. We execute 79 prompts three times each across three conditions---ChatGPT Personal (the consumer-facing default), ChatGPT Business (the organization-managed tier, which presents the same chat interface but is provisioned through an organization's workspace), and Gemini---yielding 711 total runs. ChatGPT runs are captured via network-payload instrumentation; Gemini runs via the Vertex~AI API. This design captures the full retrieval-to-citation pipeline: fan-out query generation, candidate retrieval, source selection, and claim-level attribution.

We compare LLM citations against external search-engine baselines (Bing and Google organic results) to measure overlap, visibility gaps, and position bias. We find that 78--84\% of citations overlap with conventional search indices, with position bias remaining a dominant factor: the top 1--2 search engine results account for a disproportionate share of citations, and a steep ``Page~2 cliff'' reduces visibility beyond Rank~10. However, 16--22\% of ChatGPT citations are ``invisible''---absent from both Bing and Google for the corresponding query---with frequent examples including Wikipedia, Reddit, app stores, and major tech publications such as The Verge and Wired.

A key finding from this overlap analysis is that the two ChatGPT tiers exhibit different overlap patterns with major search indices despite presenting an identical user interface. Business citations overlap with the Bing index at 81.3\% but only 27.8\% with Google, while Personal citations show 64.6\% Google overlap---a 36.8 percentage-point increase consistent with the consumer tier surfacing more Google-indexed results alongside Bing. We also discover a citation ``flip-flop'' effect: approximately 20\% of supplementary URLs are promoted to inline citations in a different run of the same query, and the structural similarity between cited and supplementary links (minimal Content DNA differentiation) suggests this boundary is near-random rather than signal-driven.

We further  develop a Content DNA enrichment pipeline (using two cross-validated LLMs as labelers) to profile the structural characteristics of approximately 6{,}500 URLs and quantify selection drift between the retrieved pool and the cited set. Both ChatGPT and Gemini exhibit statistically significant preferences for structured listicle content when compared against the search engine results baseline: comparison tables show $+$7.8\,pp lift for Gemini ($p<0.01$) and up to $+$19.7\,pp for ChatGPT ($p<0.001$), numbered lists $+$7.1 to $+$11.2\,pp, and bullet points up to $+$14.0\,pp. We then trace the pipeline one level deeper into cited listicles, extracting the individual products each model recommends and mapping them back to their position in the source listicle. This within-listicle analysis reveals strong top-rank bias (the \#1 listicle item is selected at 1.7--2.5$\times$ the random baseline across all three tiers). We also test for host bias---whether models penalize or favor a listicle author's own product---but find no strong effect.

A semantic fidelity audit of 1{,}110 solo-cited product claims, with manual verification of every flagged case, reveals that genuine attribution failures are rare: 6.4\% for Gemini and 0.7\% for GPT, with the majority of Gemini errors caused by a systematic table-grounding artefact rather than comprehension failure. These rates are substantially lower than prior work on ungrounded LLMs, reinforcing that retrieval-augmented architectures remain essential for trustworthy AI-mediated search.

These results provide an empirical foundation for Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), showing that SEO remains a prerequisite for LLM visibility while identifying structural content features, invisible-link channels, and citation stochasticity as factors that any robust GEO measurement framework must account for .
