% Abstract
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Large language models (LLMs) increasingly mediate product discovery, and which sources they cite
 directly affects brand and publisher visibility. Due to the black box nature of LLMs, the emerging 
 practice of Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) lacks the understanding that Search Engine Optimization (SEO) has built over decades.
 This thesis examines how ChatGPT and Gemini select and cite web 
 sources when answering commercial product queries. We executed 79 
 prompts three times each across three conditions (ChatGPT Plus, ChatGPT Business, and Gemini),
  yielding 711 runs. We then compared the citations in these responses against Bing and Google organic search results for the fan-out queries LLMs issued 
  behind the scenes, and profiled the structural characteristics of approximately 6{,}500 URLs.

ChatGPT Business showed 81.3\% citation overlap with Bing but only 27.8\% with Google; ChatGPT 
Plus showed substantial overlap with both (67.6\% Bing, 64.6\% Google), suggesting multi-provider retrieval. Gemini showed 77.7\% overlap with Google.
 Across all systems, 16--22\% of citations appeared in neither index, predominantly from Wikipedia, Reddit, app stores, and major tech publications. Which search index a ChatGPT tier draws from significantly affects citation behaviour: source selection drift, domain preferences, and content type distributions all differed between Business and Plus. Both ChatGPT and Gemini over-select for structured content (tables, numbered lists, bullet points) on listicles, show a preference for fresh content, and exhibit strong position bias toward the top of cited listicles. A semantic fidelity audit of over 1{,}100 product claims confirmed that genuine attribution errors were rare.

These results show that SEO remains a prerequisite for LLM visibility while identifying structural
 content features, invisible-link channels, and tier-level retrieval differences as factors that any robust GEO measurement framework must account for.
