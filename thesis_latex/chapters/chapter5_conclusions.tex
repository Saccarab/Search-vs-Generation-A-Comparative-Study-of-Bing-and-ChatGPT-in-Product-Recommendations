\section{GEO Is Mostly SEO}
\label{sec:geo-is-seo}

We ran 79 prompts three times each across ChatGPT Business,
ChatGPT Personal, and Gemini, yielding 711 runs. Across all three
conditions, 78--84\% of LLM citations trace back to a conventional
search index (Section~\ref{sec:global-overlap}). We established
that LLMs do look deeper than human users but citations from beyond
rank~10 are still quite rare (Section~\ref{sec:page-dist}) which is 
the backbone of our argument that GEO is mostly SEO.

Within the ranked results, models show consistent structural
preferences. For listicles, pages with tables, numbered lists, and
bullet points are over-selected relative to the SERP baseline. We saw a similar selection pattern on product
pages for bullet points for numbered lists (Section~\ref{sec:selection-drift}). But these features were already
standard SEO advice before GEO became a term: they help human readers
scan and compare products, and search engines have long rewarded
content that serves readers well. 
The fact that LLMs also prefer them is not necessarily a new optimization surface. 

Freshness is another converging signal: Gemini pre-filters for recency through year-token
injection in fan-out queries, and GPT shows genuine freshness
preference at the citation stage
(Section~\ref{sec:freshness-subsection}). Keeping content current has
always been good SEO practice and it is now also a good GEO practice.

Listicles are the fuel for product recommendations, and
within them. But theyu are also the page type segment we established 
that predominantly comes through SERP. So a listicle must rank to be
retrieved in the first place. A brand in theory could build a listicle blog content for ``top~10 AI tools'' 
where they carefully craft their competitor list in line with out findings. 
They could place their real competitors down in the lists or don't even mention them.
Only list soft competitors or w/e id ku can reframe this sentnce. But as long as search
engines still weigh engagement signals like bounce rate and time on
page, the page will not rank or won't maintain rankings long term. 

An unranked listicle is invisible to the grounding pipeline regardless of how well it is structured
or how well it is playing the LLM manipulation game. The
GEO optimization relies on SEO optimization loop. Our findings show us that the best way for AI visibility is to build
comprehensive content that serves human readers, earn the rankings,
and the LLM citations follow.

important for regular SEO strategies for a while. Localizing your website has been quite important for a while

even if you are a foreign language business. And the other way also applies, a English only domain also needs to have foreign language pages to maintain their access 
to English speaking audience who live in foreign language speaking countries or even people that are potentially just searching for a foreign language concept. And
even at the most extreme they'd be securing their AI visibility slot when LLMs just decides to hallucinate a foreign language fanout query.  

The workflow that of identify the prompts you rank for that GEO platforms are beginning to offer 
needs to be complemented by an understanding of fan out queries.
Once a brand understands fan out queries that LLMs responses the intent 
they can execute keyword gap analysis on these which would just mirror traditional SEO gap analysis.

The one partial exception is the invisible-link channel: 16--22\% of
citations point to URLs that do not appear in either Bing or Google
for the corresponding query (Section~\ref{sec:invisible-links}),
concentrated in Wikipedia, Reddit, app stores, and major tech
publications. These sources enter the response through pathways that seem 
to bypass search results. They give brands marketing power that can be permanent and hard to contest by their competitors
assuming OpenAI maintains their current workflow on how they ground responses.

The entire chain --- query generation, retrieval, source selection,
product extraction --- is grounded in the same web infrastructure that
SEO has optimized for decades. A brand that already runs rigorous SEO
gap analysis is, in practice, already covering most of the GEO gap.

This thesis opened with the observation that commercial queries

The trajectory points to more retrieval, not less. 


Semantic fidelity is already high when using RAG 
for commercial queries and will get better --- fewer than 2\% genuine attribution
errors --- and commercial domains produce abundant, frequently updated
content for models to ground on. As retrieval strengthens and trigger
rates rise, this fidelity will only improve. 

Browsing is
exponentially cheaper than inference
(Section~\ref{sec:rag-economics}), and the economic pressure on model
providers favours smaller models paired with heavier retrieval over
brute-force parametric generation. As retrieval expands to cover more
query types and more turns per conversation, the dependence on search
indices will deepen, not diminish. If today's search trigger
rates represent the floor rather than the ceiling, GEO does not
diverge from SEO over time --- it converges further as serach gains more importance. # maybe one is duplciat ehere between als t2 use whiever sounds better

At the same time, studying these systems is getting harder. During the
course of this research, Microsoft retired the Bing Search API
(Section~\ref{sec:structural-pivot}), Google has limited organic
results to approximately 100 per query, and the third-party scraping
services that fill the gap face active litigation from both Google
\parencite{google2025serpapi} and Reddit. The infrastructure for
independent research into LLM grounding behaviour is eroding just as
the commercial stakes are rising. The empirical data documented in
this thesis captures a snapshot of a system in rapid flux; replicating
or extending this work will require either new access agreements with
search providers or increasingly creative instrumentation.
