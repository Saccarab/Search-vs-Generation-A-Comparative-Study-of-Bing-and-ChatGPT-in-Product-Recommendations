% Abstract
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Large language models (LLMs) increasingly mediate product discovery, and which sources they cite directly affects brand and publisher visibility. Due to the black box nature of LLMs, the emerging practice of Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) lacks the understanding that Search Engine Optimization (SEO) has built over decades.
This thesis sheds light on the web search grounding behavior of ChatGPT and Gemini in commercial product recommendations, examining how they select and cite sources when answering commercial intent queries. We executed 79 prompts (drawn from real user queries) three times each across three conditions: ChatGPT Plus, ChatGPT Business, and Gemini, yielding 711 total runs.

We then compared the citation links found in LLM responses in these runs against Bing and Google organic search results for the same search queries
LLMs issued behind the scenes to measure overlap, visibility gaps, and position bias. ChatGPT Business showed 81.3\% overlap with Bing but only 27.8\% with Google. ChatGPT Plus showed substantial overlap with both: 67.6\% with Bing and 64.6\% with Google, suggesting multi-provider retrieval. Gemini showed 77.7\% overlap with Google. Across all systems, 16--22\% of citations appeared in neither index. These were predominantly from Wikipedia, Reddit, app stores, and major tech publications such as The Verge and Wired, suggesting that presence in these sources provides AI visibility that bypasses regular search engine competition.

We further developed a Content DNA enrichment pipeline to profile the structural characteristics of approximately 6{,}500 URLs. We examined whether models prefer specific page structures such as tables, bullet points, and numbered lists on product pages or listicles. Both ChatGPT and Gemini exhibit statistically significant preferences for structured content: for listicles, tables, numbered lists, and bullet points were over-selected; for product pages, numbered lists showed similar effects. Both systems also show a preference for fresh content. We then investigated whether a product's position in a listicle affects selection and found a strong position bias toward the top of the listicle. We also conducted a semantic fidelity audit of over 1{,}100 product claims in these listicles, analyzing how accurately LLMs cite what they see on the page. Genuine attribution errors were rare, confirming that retrieval-augmented grounding substantially reduces hallucination risk.

These results show that SEO remains a prerequisite for LLM visibility while identifying structural content features, invisible-link channels, and external listicle citations as factors that any robust GEO measurement framework must account for.
